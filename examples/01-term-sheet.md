# Example 01 · "Should I take this term sheet?"

> A real-world walk-through of the compass in action.

---

## The decision

**Founder asks:**

> "I have a $2M seed term sheet from a Tier-1 VC at $12M post. Liquidation preference 1x non-participating. Standard pro-rata. They want 1 board seat (they have 1, I have 2, no independent). Their partner is a former Stripe PM, very supportive, but the firm has fired 2 founders in last 18 months. We're 5 months runway. Should I take it?"

---

## Compass output

```markdown
📚 **Fundraising stack analysis · "Take $2M @ $12M post from Tier-1 VC?"**

**Cap table impact (十亿教训 #33):**
- Pre-money $10M, raising $2M = ~17% dilution
- Assuming founders held ~85% pre-money → post-money ~70%
- This is BELOW the healthy seed benchmark (65-70% lower bound)
- → Marginally acceptable, but margin is thin for Series A round

**Partner quality (Horowitz #29):**
- ✅ Ex-Stripe PM = operator background, likely understands product
- ⚠️ Firm fired 2 founders in 18 months = strong "wartime" signal
- 🚨 You said firm, not partner. Critical: did THIS partner fire them, 
  or were they other partners? Get specifics before signing.

**Valuation vs runway (Thiel #21):**
- $2M @ $12M post = high-quality seed
- But 5 months runway = you're negotiating from weakness
- Better: $2.5M at $11M post = lower valuation, more runway, 
  same dilution, less Series A pressure

**⚠️ Dependency check (大败局 II #32):**
- 5 months runway = HIGH dependency
- This term sheet is a lifeline, not an opportunity
- Negative leverage will show in negotiation

**Predatory terms scan (Doerr #97 OKR-able):**
- ✅ 1x non-participating liquidation preference (standard)
- ✅ Standard pro-rata
- 🚨 Board structure: 1 VC + 2 founder + 0 independent = imbalanced
  - With 2 founders aligned, VC can't outvote you on day 1
  - BUT if cofounders disagree, VC becomes tiebreaker
  - INSIST on 1 independent board seat at Series A

---

🚦 **Verdict:** TAKE with 3 conditions met:
1. Reference-call 2 of the 2 fired founders (not VC's intros)
2. Negotiate either (a) $2.5M @ $11M for more runway OR 
   (b) add independent board seat trigger at Series A
3. Confirm your supportive partner is on YOUR board, not a junior

**Red lines (walk if any present):**
- Fired founders report partner was hostile / unsupportive
- VC refuses ANY of the 3 conditions above
- Their pro-rata exceeds 100% of round (would block other VCs)

**Negotiation moves (48h):**
1. Email: "We're excited. Two asks: (a) $2.5M to give us 18-month runway; 
   (b) trigger for independent board seat at $X Series A milestone."
2. Reference calls scheduled with 2 fired founders
3. Backup conversation with 1 alternative VC to preserve leverage
```

---

## Why this output works

**Multi-frame stacking:**
- Cap table math (十亿教训) + Partner quality (Horowitz) + Valuation theory (Thiel) + Survival math (大败局 II) + Terms compliance (Doerr) = 5 independent angles

**Productive disagreement:**
- Thiel says "lower valuation can be better" → conflicts with founder's instinct
- 大败局 II says "5 months runway = negative leverage" → forces honesty about negotiation position

**Failure case integration:**
- 十亿教训 cap table benchmarks are not theoretical — they come from observing founders who lost control

**Commit to direction:**
- Not "it depends on your conviction" → "TAKE with 3 conditions"
- Names 3 specific red lines, not vague concerns

**Concrete 48-hour next steps:**
- Email draft, reference calls, backup VC — all actionable

---

## What a generic answer looks like (and why it fails)

```
"$12M post is reasonable for seed. Make sure the partner is supportive 
and the terms are clean. Trust your gut."
```

This is the difference between **advice** and **a compass**. Advice tells you what to feel. A compass tells you which direction to walk.
